1 · Academic tenses In sample quiz

Present simple

For facts that are true now, general truths, and habits.

  • Video games are currently one of the mass entertainment media…
  • Young people devote a large part of their time…
  • Also used to report what research shows in general: Research… shows that…

Signal words: currently, now, generally, in general, usually.

Present perfect (has / have + past participle)

For something that started in the past and still matters now, and for research trends up to the present.

  • …their possible educational uses have come to the fore.
  • Most studies on DGBL have [examined] its cognitive benefits…

Signal words: in recent years, over the past decade, so far, to date, recently.

Past simple

For finished actions at a specific time, such as what a particular study did or found, and what you did in your own methods.

  • The Statistical Office of the European Union revealed that…
  • We eliminated 25 participants…

Signal words: in 2020, last year, a specific named study, "in this study we…"

Subject–verb agreement traps

  • "which" referring to a whole idea takes a singular verb: …to this use, which explains…
  • "as well as" does not make the subject plural. Find the main noun: the implications… as well as their uses, have come (plural because "implications" is plural).
  • "Research" is uncountable, so it takes a singular verb: Research… shows.
  • "The VG, in general" is treated as one medium in the key, so the verb is singular: has. Ignore the comma phrase "in general" when finding the subject.

2 · Active and passive voice In sample quiz

How to transform, in 4 steps

  1. Find the object of the active sentence. It becomes the new subject.
  2. Keep the same tense: put be in that tense.
  3. Add the past participle of the main verb.
  4. Drop the doer ("we", "the researchers") when it's obvious or unimportant, which is usual in Methods sections.

We devised the questionnaire. → The questionnaire was devised.

Why academic writing uses the passive

  • It keeps the focus on the research, not the researcher.
  • It sounds objective and impersonal.
  • It's standard for describing methods: what was done, not who did it.
  • "It" passive for reporting claims: The researchers have indicated that… → It has been indicated that…

Passive by tense

TensePassive formExample
Present simpleam / is / are + ppProposals are approved by the committee.
Past simplewas / were + ppThe questionnaire for teachers and students was devised.
Present perfecthas / have been + ppThese points have been treated as scalar or range variables.
Past perfecthad been + ppThe data had been cleaned before analysis.
Futurewill be + ppThe results will be analyzed.
Modalcan / may / should be + ppThese findings may be explained by…
Continuousis / was being + ppThe data are being processed.

Singular vs. plural still matters: 25 participants… were eliminated, The approval… was received.

3 · Academic style In sample quiz

Rules behind the 10 errors in the quiz

  • Don't address the reader or give commands. Imagine that… → state the fact directly.
  • Don't start a sentence with And / But / So. Use Similarly, Furthermore, However, Therefore.
  • Avoid redundant connectors. and while → while.
  • Replace phrasal verbs with one-word verbs: find out → find, use up → use, work on → examine.
  • Replace informal words: a lot of → many, just like → similar to, clear-cut → conclusive.
  • Keep tense consistent with meaning: a research trend continuing to now takes present perfect: researchers have focused on.

4 · Contractions From exam notes

Academic writing never uses contractions. Write both words in full. This is the easiest point to earn on the intro-editing question, so scan every apostrophe.

Trap: apostrophes for possession are fine: the students' responses, the study's aim. "Its" (no apostrophe) is possessive and is not a contraction: DGBL and its cognitive benefits.

5 · Hedging From exam notes

What hedging is

Hedging means softening a claim so it matches the evidence. Research rarely proves anything absolutely, so academic writers use cautious language to avoid overclaiming.

Overclaim: Video games always improve learning.
Hedged: Video games may improve learning in some contexts.

Also a mistake: hedging a well-established fact, or stacking hedges: It might possibly perhaps suggest…. One hedge per claim is usually enough.

Hedging toolkit

TypeExamples
Modal verbsmay, might, can, could
Cautious verbssuggest, indicate, appear, seem, tend to
Adverbspossibly, probably, likely, generally, often
Adjectivespossible, probable, likely
Quantity limitssome, many, most (instead of all)
Frequency limitsoften, usually (instead of always / never)
PhrasesIt is likely that…, The findings suggest that…

Words to catch and replace: prove → suggest / indicate, always → often / tend to, never → rarely, all → most / many, It is clear that → It appears that, definitely / certainly → likely.

6 · Combining voice and tense From exam notes

Your exam's first question combines both skills in one passage. For each blank, make two decisions:

  1. Voice. Is the subject doing the action (active) or receiving it (passive)? Data collect… Data can't collect anything, so it's passive: Data were collected. Participants complete a questionnaire They do it, so it's active: Participants completed.
  2. Tense. Use the signal words: a finished action in your study → past; a general truth → present; a trend up to now ("to date", "in recent years") → present perfect.

Then check agreement: the questionnaire was vs. the questionnaires were.

Suggestions

  • Time yourself. The sample quiz was 25 minutes for 3 questions and the exam is 30, so roughly 10 minutes per question. Try Practice A, B and D back to back in 30 minutes.
  • Editing questions don't tell you the error type. Make three passes over the intro: one for apostrophes (contractions), one for absolute words like always, prove, clear, all (hedges), and one for informal words, phrasal verbs and sentence openers.

  • Ask your instructor whether the combined question gives you the voice (e.g. "(collect, passive)") or expects you to decide it. The practice here makes you decide, which is the harder version.
